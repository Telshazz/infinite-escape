## 1. What Is Infinite Escape?

Infinite Escape is an **AI-powered mixed reality escape room operating
system**. A physical venue builds its rooms once — walls, furniture
stations, fans, misters, lights, and speakers. Everything else — the story,
the puzzles, the atmosphere, the pacing, even which door leads where — is
generated by software and can be completely swapped between sessions.

This demo puts you *inside* that experience in your browser: a
first-person, fully playable walkthrough of a three-chamber facility that
can become seven completely different escape rooms at the press of a button.

**One physical build. Unlimited realities.**

## 2. Quick Start — Your First Session

1. Open the demo in **Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop**.
2. Click **Launch Investor Demo**.
3. Build your group profile (see Section 3) and click
   **Generate Room Experience**.
4. Watch the AI resolve your session — every line shown is a real decision
   it just made about your game.
5. Click **Enter the Room — First Person**.
6. **Click once inside the room** to take control of the camera. You're in.

Walk up to the glowing altar in front of you, press **E**, and solve your
first puzzle. From there, the room will tell you where to go next.

## 3. Building Your Group Profile

Nothing on this screen is decoration. Every choice changes the game you
get — this is the heart of the product.

### 3.1 Players

How many people are in your group. Small groups get a focused,
one-puzzle-at-a-time experience. Groups of five or more unlock **parallel
stations** — extra puzzles placed so your team can split up and work
simultaneously, just like a real escape room crew.

### 3.2 Difficulty (1–10)

Difficulty changes *what gets built*, not just how cryptic the clues are:

| Band | Levels | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Guided** | 1–3 | One puzzle per room, no decoy pieces, unlimited hints, a Game Master who steps in when you're stuck |
| **Standard** | 4–6 | Up to two puzzles per room, light decoys, 5 hints per puzzle |
| **Hard** | 7–8 | Up to three puzzles per room, decoys everywhere, clues that live in *other rooms*, only 2 hints per puzzle |
| **Expert** | 9–10 | Everything the theme has, cross-room dependencies, 1 hint per puzzle, a Game Master who stays silent unless asked |

### 3.3 Game Mode

The mode sets a *ceiling* on how intense the experience is allowed to get:

- **Adventure** — the default. Balanced atmosphere.
- **Family Friendly** — hard-caps scare-adjacent effects no matter what
  else you pick, removes startle moments entirely, and replaces the
  countdown timer with a relaxed count-up clock.
- **Horror** — raises the baseline tension: deeper shadows, flickering
  lights, a darker-voiced Game Master.
- **Corporate Team Building** — biases toward split-up-and-collaborate
  puzzle layouts and keeps the Game Master's tone professional.

### 3.4 Fear Level (None / Mild / Intense)

An independent dial for atmosphere *within* your mode's ceiling. Horror +
None gives you tense-but-gentle; Horror + Intense arms randomized startle
moments. Family Friendly + Intense still stays family friendly — **the mode
always wins**.

### 3.5 Session Length (30 / 45 / 60 minutes)

Length controls how much of the facility opens up. A 30-minute session
seals off a chamber and rebuilds the route around it — a short Expert
session is *fewer rooms with denser puzzles*, not a rushed long one.

## 4. The Generation Sequence

After you click Generate, the AI Game Master resolves your profile into a
concrete session plan. The panel shows its actual output: how many chambers
are active, how many puzzles were placed, your hint budget, effect
intensity, and the Game Master's posture. This is the system showing its
work — the same room genuinely becomes a different game for different
groups.

## 5. Playing the Room

### 5.1 Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| **Click** | Take control of the camera / interact |
| **W A S D** | Move |
| **Mouse** | Look around |
| **Shift** | Sprint |
| **C** | Crouch |
| **E** | Examine / open / inspect whatever you're looking at |
| **Esc** | Step back from a puzzle, or pause |
| **H** | Ask the Game Master for a hint |
| **V** | Switch reality layer (see Section 8) |
| **G** | Show/hide the Game Master panel |

### 5.2 Moving Around

You walk at real human speed through rooms built at real scale — the
chambers are 16–24 feet across, doors are door-height, and tables are
table-height. You can't walk through furniture or walls; the world is
physically solid.

### 5.3 Examining & Solving Puzzles

Aim the center dot at anything interesting. If it can be used, it
highlights and shows a prompt like **[E] Examine Atlantean Navigation
Altar**. Press E and the camera glides in close — the puzzle appears as a
holographic panel projected off the object itself. Solve it there, or press
Esc to step back and explore more first.

### 5.4 Doors and Locked Passages

Doors are sealed until the room says otherwise. Aim at a door to see its
status — a locked door tells you what still stands in your way (for
example, *"The passage does not answer. The Navigation Altar must burn
first."*). When you open an unlocked door, reality reconfigures around you
and you step into the next chamber.

You can always walk **back** through doors you've opened.

### 5.5 Clues Hidden in Other Rooms

At Hard and Expert difficulty, some puzzles deliberately withhold their
answer — the legend, code, or passphrase is written on a mural, whiteboard,
or plaque in a *different* room. Inspect suspicious wall art (press E on
it) and remember what it says. Backtracking is part of the game.

## 6. The Five Puzzle Types

Every theme builds its puzzles from five core mechanisms, each dressed
completely differently per world:

1. **Sequence** — activate symbols in the correct order. *(Atlantis's
   sacred altar; the space station's reactor cold-start.)*
2. **Cipher Dial** — rotate wheels to the correct combination. *(A relic
   archive's numbers; a pirate compass's bearings; a badge keypad.)*
3. **Connect** — wire the left side to the right side correctly. *(Reroute
   temple energy; patch a corporate network; re-arm a generator.)*
4. **Arrange** — place the right item in the right slot. *(Hang heraldic
   crests; assemble a torn sea-chart; load a centrifuge.)*
5. **Riddle** — answer a question in your own words. *(A stone leviathan's
   question; a station AI's test; the Captain's riddle.)*

Wrong attempts shake the mechanism and reset it — watch for decoy pieces
that belong to no solution at all.

## 7. Your AI Game Master

### 7.1 The GM Panel

The drawer on the right (toggle with **G**) is your Game Master: current
objective, a live map of every puzzle and which room it's in, your group
profile, and a running narration of everything happening in the session.

### 7.2 Asking for Hints

Press **H** anytime, or use the button in the GM panel or inside any
puzzle. Hints target whatever you're working on.

### 7.3 How Hints Adapt to You

Two systems work together:

- **Quality** — your first hint is subtle; ask again and hints get
  progressively more direct until the answer is nearly spelled out.
- **Quantity** — your difficulty sets the budget: unlimited on Guided,
  5 per puzzle on Standard, 2 on Hard, 1 on Expert. When a puzzle's budget
  is spent, the Game Master says so — in character.

On Guided difficulty the Game Master also watches for you being stuck and
volunteers a nudge after about a minute of no progress.

## 8. The Reality Switch

The toggle in the bottom-left corner (or press **V**) is the signature
demo moment — three views of the *same physical space*:

- **Physical** — the real room an operator actually builds: plain gray
  walls, blank furniture stations, visible fans, misters, LED channels,
  and speakers.
- **Mixed Reality** — what players experience: the full themed world
  layered over that same hardware.
- **Mapping** — the X-ray view: every physical object labeled with the
  digital twin it's currently playing. The fan is an "Ocean-Current
  Emitter" in Atlantis and a "Ventilation Failure Duct" in the zombie lab —
  same fan.

---

## 9. The Environmental Effects Rig

The **FX** button (top-right) opens the effects rail — six channels of
physical hardware the software choreographs: **Wind, Mist, Lighting,
Audio, Scent, Vibration**. Toggle them live and watch the room respond:
fans spin, mist rises, energy channels pulse, positional sound moves around
you. Solving puzzles also fires effects automatically as story beats.

How hard these effects push is decided by your profile — Mode sets the
ceiling, Fear Level tunes within it, Difficulty sets the baseline.

## 10. The Seven Worlds

| World | Structure | Flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **The Lost City of Atlantis** | Linear, 3 chambers | A drowned temple waking after 3,000 years |
| **Pirate Adventure** | Linear | A cursed galleon hold, one tide from sinking |
| **Zombie Apocalypse** | Linear | A quarantine lab, sixty seconds after containment failed |
| **Alice in Wonderland** | **Non-linear hub** — side chambers in any order | A tea party where logic runs backwards |
| **Space Station** | Linear | A derelict orbital lab drifting toward re-entry |
| **Medieval Castle** | Linear | The king's vault, guarded by riddles older than the crown |
| **Corporate Team Building** | Linear, parallel-heavy | A boardroom heist with KPIs on the line |

Every world assigns the five puzzle mechanisms differently — switching
themes changes the *gameplay*, not just the paint.

## 11. Regenerating the Room

From the pause menu (or after escaping), choose **Regenerate Room** and
pick any world. In seconds, the same three chambers — same walls, same
furniture stations, same hardware — become a different story with a
different puzzle graph. This is the product's core promise performed live:
zero construction, zero downtime, zero new floor space.

---

## 12. Escaping — Finishing a Session

Solve every mechanism the session placed, then find the exit — the portal
gate, the quarterdeck hatch, the executive badge door. If it's still
sealed, it will name what's missing. Open it, and you've escaped. The
completion banner offers the two best next moves: regenerate into a new
world, or open the business case.

---

## 13. The Pause Menu

Press **Esc** while walking around. You'll get the full control reference,
your session profile, and buttons for **Regenerate Room**, **Investor
View**, and **Exit Demo**. Click **Resume** to jump back in.

## 14. For Investors & Operators

Open **Investor View** from the pause menu or completion banner. Alongside
the commercial model and single-room ROI, the *Architecture Is the Moat*
section shows the split that makes this business work:

- **Fixed layer (built once):** 3 chambers, 5 doorways, anchor stations,
  effects hardware.
- **Swappable layer (regenerated per session):** theme, story, room-graph
  shape, puzzle assignments, difficulty, pacing, effect choreography.

It also reports the live session's actual stats — chambers, puzzles,
difficulty band, and how content was paced to your group size.

## 15. Troubleshooting & FAQ

**I clicked but the mouse won't look around.**
Click directly on the 3D view once. Your browser will "lock" the pointer to
the game. Esc always releases it.

**A puzzle panel is open and I want to leave it.**
Press Esc, or click the ✕ in its corner.

**The exit door won't open.**
Aim at it — it names exactly which mechanisms remain. Check the GM panel's
puzzle map for anything unsolved, including in earlier rooms.

**I hear synthesized ambience instead of recorded sound.**
That's the built-in fallback. Operators can drop recorded ambience files
into the audio folder and they take over automatically per theme.

**Performance is rough on my machine.**
Add `?nofx` to the URL to disable the cinematic post-processing chain.

**Some props look simpler than others.**
Hero props stream from a generation service; if your network blocks them,
the demo silently substitutes its built-in versions. Nothing breaks.

**Is my progress saved?**
Sessions are intentionally disposable — that's the pitch. Regenerate and
play again; it's a different game.

## 16. System Requirements

- **Desktop or laptop** — this is deliberately not a mobile experience.
- **Chrome or Edge** (latest), Firefox supported.
- A GPU from roughly the last 6 years; integrated graphics work with
  `?nofx`.
- Mouse strongly recommended (trackpads work, mice feel better).
- Audio on — the Game Master and the room have things to tell you.
